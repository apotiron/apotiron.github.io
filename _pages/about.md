I'm Alien 👽, coming from the microbiology planet to visit the philosophy of science universe. My research focuses on the **conceptual foundations** and **societal implications of microbiology**, bridging microbiology, philosophy, and history to address overlooked issues in human and planetary health. I use a **practice-oriented**, interdisciplinary approach where scientific practice inform philosophical inquiry and vice versa.

Relying on my experience in the lab on microbial communities 🦠 and through microbial ecology and microbiome research case studies, I address philosophical issues while also aiming at critically analysing microbiology to improve its practice. I developed a novel philosophical account of biological samples. I proposed a model-based approach to diversity measurement suggesting that microbiologists should choose diversity concepts appropriate to their research questions, so they can calibrate and improve their measurements. I study how microbiology’s history developed epistemic, methodological and ontological commitments that can obstruct contemporary integration even when researchers share the ambition to collaborate. 

As part of the [Microbiome research and race in the 'Local South'](https://www.uu.nl/en/research/history-and-philosophy-of-the-life-sciences/microbiome-research-and-race-in-the-local-south) project, I investigate the role of 'race' and other ethno-racial labels in scientific inferences, their definitions, historical development and their contemporary use in microbiome research with a focus on the Latin American context. I use innovative conceptual mapping methods and foster interdisciplinary dialogue to examine the field's ethical and epistemic assumptions. My aim is to propose conceptual solutions grounded in scientific practice to help scientists avoid racism.
