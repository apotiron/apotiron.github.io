I'm Alien 👽, coming from the microbiology planet to visit the philosophy of science universe. 

My research focuses on the **conceptual foundations** and **societal implications of microbiology**, bridging microbiology, philosophy, and history to address overlooked issues in human and planetary 🌍 health. I use a **practice-oriented**, interdisciplinary approach where scientific practice informs philosophical inquiry and vice versa.

Through microbial ecology and microbiome research case studies 🦠, I address philosophical issues while critically analysing microbiology to improve its practice. I investigate biological sample definition, microbial biodiversity measurement practices, causality and challenges to scientific integration.

I also examine how scientific categories—including race and ethno-racial descriptors—shape microbiome research and its societal impact. As part of the [Microbiome research and race in the 'Local South'](https://www.uu.nl/en/research/history-and-philosophy-of-the-life-sciences/microbiome-research-and-race-in-the-local-south) project, I use innovative conceptual mapping methods to decipher the field's ethical and epistemic assumptions in the Latin American context. My aim is to propose conceptual solutions to help scientists avoid racism.
